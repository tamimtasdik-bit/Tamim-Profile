<h3>
What Are Affidavits and Statutory Declarations? </h3>









<ol><li><strong>Affidavits</strong><ul><li>An affidavit is a written statement sworn under oath or affirmation, affirming the truthfulness of the contents.</li><li>Often used in legal proceedings, affidavits serve as evidence in court, confirming facts or information provided by the affiant (the person making the statement).</li></ul></li><li><strong>Statutory Declarations</strong><ul><li>A statutory declaration is a formal, written statement affirming that certain information is true, made in the presence of a solicitor or commissioner for oaths.</li><li>These declarations are used in non-contentious matters, such as verifying identity, certifying documents, or confirming facts for legal or administrative purposes.</li></ul></li></ol>

 

















<h6>
Common Uses for Affidavits and Statutory Declarations </h6>









<ul><li><strong>Legal Proceedings</strong>: Providing evidence in court cases or resolving disputes.</li><li><strong>Change of Name</strong>: Confirming a name change, often for official record updates.</li><li><strong>Property Matters</strong>: Declaring ownership, rights, or disputes in property transactions.</li><li><strong>Immigration Applications</strong>: Supporting visa or residency applications with detailed personal statements.</li><li><strong>Business Matters</strong>: Verifying corporate information or fulfilling regulatory requirements.</li><li><strong>Personal Matters</strong>: Confirming relationships, financial arrangements, or other factual assertions.</li></ul>
