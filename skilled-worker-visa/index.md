<div>
<div>

<h3>
<span>What Is the Skilled Worker Visa?</span> </h3>
</div>
</div>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<div>

<div>
<p>The Skilled Worker Visa allows non-UK nationals to work in the UK for an approved employer who holds a valid <strong>sponsor licence</strong>. This visa replaces the former Tier 2 (General) Work Visa and provides a route to <strong>settlement in the UK </strong>after 5 years.</p><h5><strong>Skilled Worker Visa Requirements</strong></h5><p><span><b>1. Eligibility Criteria<br/></b></span><span>To apply for a Skilled Worker visa, you must:</span></p><ul><li>Have a valid job offer from a UK employer licensed to sponsor Skilled Worker visa applicants.</li><li>Meet the skill level requirement (RQF Level 3 or equivalent).</li><li>Be paid the minimum salary or the “going rate” for the job, whichever is higher.<br/><br/><span>2<b>. </b></span><span><b>Salary Requirements</b></span></li><li>Standard salary threshold: <strong>£38,700 per year</strong> or <strong>£18.62 per hour</strong>.</li><li>New entrants, some shortage occupations, and certain health and education roles might have lower salary thresholds.<br/><br/>The UK Government is set to increase the salary threshold for Skilled Worker visas from the current £26,200 to £38,700 starting in April 2024. This adjustment is based on updated data from the Office for National Statistics (ONS) and reflects the 50th percentile of full-time earnings in 2023. Additionally, the “Shortage Occupation List” will be replaced by an “Immigration Salary List” (ISL), and the 20% salary discount previously available for shortage occupations will be discontinued</li></ul><p>Healthcare and social care workers, as well as individuals in occupations tied to national payscales (e.g., certain education roles), will be exempt from the new threshold. These changes are designed to balance the UK labour market by reducing dependency on migrant workers while still addressing critical shortages in sectors like health and social care</p><p>For those currently on the Skilled Worker route, transitional measures will apply, allowing them to change sponsors, extend their visas, or settle under the existing rules without immediately meeting the higher threshold<br/><br/><span>3. </span><span><b>English Language Requirement<br/></b></span><span>You must prove your proficiency in English by:</span></p><ul><li>Passing a Home Office-approved English language test.</li><li>Holding a degree taught in English or being from an English-speaking country.<br/><span><br/>4. </span><span><b>Tuberculosis (TB) Test<br/></b></span>Applicants from specific countries must provide a TB test certificate if they are staying for more than 6 months.<br/><span><br/>5. </span><span><b>Dependent Applications<br/></b></span>Your spouse, partner, and children can join you as dependants. They must also meet financial requirements and provide appropriate documentation.</li></ul>
</div>
</div> </div>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<div>

<div>
<h5><strong>Maintenance Funds</strong></h5><p>For the UK Skilled Worker visa, applicants must meet specific maintenance fund requirements to demonstrate they can financially support themselves upon arrival without relying on public funds. The primary applicant must show they have at least <strong>£1,270</strong> available, held in a bank account for 28 consecutive days, ending no more than 31 days before the application submission date</p><p><strong>Additional Requirements for Dependents:</strong></p><ul><li><strong>£285</strong> for a dependent partner.</li><li><strong>£315</strong> for the first dependent child.</li><li><strong>£200</strong> for each additional child.</li></ul><p><strong>Exemptions:</strong></p><ol><li><strong>Employer Certification</strong>: An A-rated sponsor can certify maintenance on the Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS), meaning the applicant doesn’t need to provide personal financial evidence.</li><li><strong>Existing UK Residents</strong>: If the applicant has been lawfully living in the UK for at least 12 months, they are exempt from providing maintenance fund evidence</li><li>Applicants can rely on their own funds or those of a partner (if applying together) or their employer’s certification. However, funds held in unregulated accounts or non-traditional assets like stocks or cryptocurrency are not acceptable</li></ol>
</div>
</div> </div>

<div>
<h6>
<span>Can You Bring Dependants on a Skilled Worker Visa?</span> </h6>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</section>
<div>
<div>
<p>Yes, you can bring dependants, including:</p><ul><li>Your spouse, civil partner, or unmarried partner.</li><li>Children under 18 years of age.<br/>Each dependant will need to meet specific requirements, including financial maintenance and TB testing.</li></ul><p><strong>Benefits of the Skilled Worker Visa</strong></p><ul><li><strong>Pathway to Settlement</strong>: You can apply for indefinite leave to remain (ILR) after 5 years.</li><li><strong>Work Flexibility</strong>: Switch jobs or employers within the same visa category.</li><li><strong>Bring Family Members</strong>: Your family can live and work in the UK.</li><li><strong>Access to Healthcare and Education</strong>: Dependants can benefit from free healthcare and education.</li></ul> </div>

<h3>
<span>FAQs About the Skilled Worker Visa</span> </h3>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</section>
<div>
<div>
<div>

<div>
<ol><li><strong>Can I extend my Graduate Visa?</strong><br/>No, the Graduate Visa cannot be extended. However, you may switch to other visa categories, such as the Skilled Worker Visa, if eligible.</li><li><strong> Can I study on a Graduate Visa?</strong><br/>Yes, you can study, but it cannot be a course sponsored by a student sponsor.</li><li><strong> What happens if my Graduate Visa application is refused?</strong><br/>You may have the right to appeal or request an administrative review. Our solicitors can assist you in challenging a refusal.</li></ol>
