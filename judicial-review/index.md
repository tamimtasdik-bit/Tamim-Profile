<h3>
<span>Judicial Review: Challenging Immigration Decisions in the UK</span> </h3>
</div>
</div>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<div>

<div>
<p>If you have faced a <strong>refusal</strong> from the <strong>Home Office</strong> and do not have the right to appeal, you may still have the option to challenge the decision through <strong>judicial review</strong>. A <strong>judicial review</strong> is a legal process where the <strong>High Court</strong> examines the lawfulness of a decision made by the Home Office or other public bodies. This process is available if you believe the decision was unlawful, unreasonable, or procedurally unfair.</p>
</div>
</div> </div>
</div>

<div>

<h3>
<span>What Is Judicial Review?</span> </h3>
</div>
</div>
</div>
<div>
<div>
<div>

<div>
<p>Judicial review is not a way to challenge the substance or the merits of a Home Office decision; instead, it focuses on whether the decision-making process was <strong>lawful</strong> and whether the correct procedures were followed. The court does not redecide the case, but rather examines if the decision was made in accordance with the law.</p><p>Common grounds for a judicial review include:</p><ul><li><strong>Unlawfulness</strong>: The decision maker did not have the legal authority to make the decision.</li><li><strong>Irrationality</strong>: The decision is so unreasonable that no reasonable authority would have made it.</li><li><strong>Procedural unfairness</strong>: The decision was made without following the correct procedures or failing to take into account relevant factors.</li></ul>
</div>
</div> </div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<h6>
<span>The Judicial Review Process</span> </h6>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</section>
<div>
<div>
<ol><li><strong>Pre-Action Protocol</strong>: Before applying for judicial review, you are required to send a <strong>pre-action letter</strong> to the Home Office, outlining the grounds for your challenge. This gives the Home Office a chance to reconsider the decision.</li><li><strong>Application for Permission</strong>: After the pre-action stage, you can apply for <strong>permission</strong> to bring the judicial review. The High Court will consider whether your case has sufficient merit to proceed. If permission is granted, the case moves forward to the substantive hearing.</li><li><strong>The Hearing</strong>: If permission is granted, a <strong>judge</strong> will review the case in detail, and both parties will present their arguments. The judge will then make a ruling, either dismissing the case or ordering the Home Office to reconsider its decision.</li><li><strong>Outcome</strong>: If the judicial review is successful, the Home Office may be required to <strong>reconsider</strong> its decision. However, the Home Office may also appeal the decision, which could further delay the outcome.</li></ol> </div>

<h6>
<span>FAQs About the Graduate Visa</span> </h6>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
</section>
<div>
<div>
<div>

<div>
<p><strong>1. Is the Time Limit for Applying for Judicial Review?<br/></strong>The time limit for applying for <strong>judicial review</strong> is typically <strong>three months</strong> from the date of the decision being challenged. However, in certain cases, the time limit can be shorter. It is essential to act quickly if you wish to challenge a Home Office decision.<br/><br/><strong>2. Can I Apply for Judicial Review If I Have a Right of Appeal?<br/></strong>If you have the right of appeal, you are generally required to use the appeal process first. Judicial review is typically only available when no right of appeal exists or when the appeal process has been exhausted.<br/><br/><strong>3. What Happens If My Judicial Review Is Successful?<br/></strong>If your judicial review is successful, the Home Office may be required to reconsider the decision and make a new decision in accordance with the law. In some cases, the Home Office may be ordered to pay your legal costs.<br/><br/><strong>4. Can Judicial Review Be Used to Challenge Any Immigration Decision?<br/></strong>Judicial review can be used to challenge various Home Office decisions, including <strong>visa refusals</strong>, <strong>immigration status decisions</strong>, and <strong>deportation orders</strong>, provided there are grounds for arguing that the decision was unlawful.<br/><br/><strong>5. How Much Does Judicial Review Cost?<br/></strong>The cost of a <strong>judicial review</strong> can vary depending on the complexity of the case. We offer competitive pricing and can provide you with a clear understanding of the costs involved at the outset of your case.</p>
